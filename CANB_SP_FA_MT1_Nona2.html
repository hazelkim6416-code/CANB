Lou Gehrig’s disease damaged Hawking’s body parts that control muscles. Unable to control his muscles, Hawking couldn’t walk or move by himself. His body became slouched, and his head was tilted to the side. At first, he needed crutches to help him walk. Eventually, he had to use a wheelchair to get around.

Moving wasn’t the only problem for Hawking, to make matters worse, he had problems speaking, too. He couldn’t control the muscles in his mouth and throat. This made his speech unclear. Later, he couldn’t talk at all and had to use a special computer to speak.

Doctors didn’t have much hope for Hawking. They thought that he would only live a couple more years. Hawking showed them they were wrong, though. He lived an active life for more than 50 years until he passed away at age 76.

U1 L2

Lou Gehrig’s disease made Stephen Hawking’s body very weak and fragile. He became unable to move or talk by himself. Despite this tragic disease, his mind stayed strong, and he accomplished great things in science.

Hawking was one of the world’s greatest scientists. He spent his life solving the mysteries of the universe. He wrote many important papers during his career. For his work, he received many awards and honors.

Hawking was also a popular public speaker. Despite his disability, he spoke around the world. He explained difficult scientific concepts in ways people could understand. He talked about the universe in his lectures. Black holes and the Big Bang were his favorite topics.

Hawking was a best-selling writer, too. His books still make a lot of people interested in science. “A Brief History of Time,” his first book, has sold more than 10 million copies. Hawking even wrote children’s books about the universe.

During an interview, Hawking showed how he felt about his disease. “It is a waste of time to be angry about my disability. One has to get on with life, and I haven’t done badly,” he said. Hawking lived with Lou Gehrig’s disease for 55 years until he died in 2018. During his life, he never gave up on his work. Thanks to Hawking, people now have a much better understanding of the universe.

When do you think people first began writing? Over 5,000 years ago, people living in Sumer (today’s Iraq) created the first writing system.

Most Sumerians were farmers. They made a living by trading grain and animals. At first, they had no writing system. They couldn’t record how much grain and how many animals they traded.

Then, Sumerians started drawing pictures on clay. With these pictures, they could keep track of their trades. Their lives became much easier.

Later, people began to think drawing pictures wasn’t convenient. People drew pictures differently. Also, some took a long time to draw. Therefore, Sumerians created the first writing system called cuneiform. Cuneiform changed their writing from pictures to wedge forms. It made writing easier and faster.

The symbol for “fish” shows an example of this change. Before cuneiform, the symbol was a picture of a fish. With cuneiform, the symbol became a series of wedges. It didn’t look like a fish, but it was more convenient to write.

Over the years, other cultures also used cuneiform to write. However, there were some words that couldn’t be written in cuneiform. It became extinct about 2,000 years ago.

Cuneiform was used for thousands of years until a new writing system replaced it. The new writing system was called an alphabet. It was invented about 4,000 years ago in Phoenicia (today’s Lebanon).

The first alphabet was made up of 22 letters. Each letter had one sound. By using different letter combinations, any sound could be written. The letters were just simple lines and they were easy to write.

The first alphabet was soon adapted by the Phoenicians’ neighbors. A) It was much more convenient than cuneiform. B) Phoenicians loved cuneiform. C) Many cultures took the first alphabet and changed it. D) They created their own versions of alphabets throughout history.

Different alphabets are in use today. The most widely used is the Latin alphabet. It has 26 letters. About 120 languages use it. English, Spanish, and French are some of the major ones. The second most used alphabet is Arabic, which has 28 letters.

Many Asian and African languages use it. The Cyrillic alphabet, the third most used, has 33 letters. About 10 languages use it, including Russian and Mongolian.

Writing systems have developed a lot over thousands of years. Do you think people will still be using today’s writing systems in the next millennium?
